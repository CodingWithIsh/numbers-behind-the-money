α, β and γ Trianguli

Where the three stars of the Triangle are, how far they are from Earth and how they move, from ESA's Gaia space telescope (Data Release 3, June 2022). Gaia gives a complete record for γ Trianguli. For α and β Trianguli, two of the brightest stars Gaia observed, it publishes only a position. Their distances and motions here come from the earlier Hipparcos mission.

In space, relative to Earth

Earth sits at the centre and each line is our line of sight to a star. The Triangle we see is a trick of perspective: β Trianguli is twice as far away as α. Axes are in light years. X points to right ascension 0h on the celestial equator, Y to 6h, and Z to the north celestial pole.

Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, and hover over a star for its X, Y and Z coordinates.

Distance and motion

Distance from Earth

Distance = 1 / parallax. The open circle is the Hipparcos distance for γ Trianguli, shown for comparison with Gaia's.

Proper motion

Each arrow shows the direction and speed of a star's drift across the sky, in milliarcseconds per year. East is left and north is up.
